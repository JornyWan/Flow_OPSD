\begin{abstract}
On-policy distillation (OPD) has recently emerged as an efficient paradigm for
multi-task alignment of diffusion models: task-specific teachers supervise a
single student along its own rollout trajectory through a closed-form,
low-variance per-step velocity-matching loss. In the multi-teacher setting,
DiffusionOPD sums the per-teacher losses uniformly, which drives the student
toward the per-step \emph{mean} of the teacher velocity fields. We revisit this
choice through the lens of multi-objective optimization. Our first contribution
is a rigorous theory: the multi-teacher OPD losses are values of a \emph{single}
functional---squared velocity error in a shared isotropic output metric,
evaluated at shared on-policy states---so they are commensurable across teachers,
and adaptive weighting is well-posed in a way it is not for generic multi-task
learning. We show that the uniform mean is Pareto-optimal in \emph{loss} space
(so the naive convex-hull argument does not apply), and locate the real
difficulty in the nonlinear loss$\to$reward map, under which the loss-sum
minimizer can be reward-dominated. We then propose \textbf{CA-OPD}, a
lightweight conflict-averse reweighting that replaces the uniform sum with an
entropy-regularized (temperature-$\tau$) Tchebycheff min-max over EMA teacher
losses; an envelope-theorem argument shows the detached-weight gradient is
\emph{exact} for the smooth surrogate, and $\tau\to\infty$ recovers uniform OPD.
Empirically, on a faithful reproduction of DiffusionOPD (SD3.5-Medium, three
teachers) whose numbers match the original, CA-OPD improves \emph{convergence
efficiency} on the lagging (aesthetics) axis---reaching the uniform baseline's
best aesthetic score roughly 180 steps earlier---and exposes $\tau$ as a knob
trading convergence speed against final quality. Honestly, on this near-saturated
three-teacher benchmark CA-OPD does \emph{not} improve final quality---at
convergence it is within single-seed noise of the uniform baseline on every reward
axis and higher on none. We further ablate four
principled ideas (min-max, Huber-robust, Min-SNR weighting, SDE$\to$ODE
annealing), each with a mechanistic explanation, mapping the design space of OPD
improvements.
\end{abstract}
