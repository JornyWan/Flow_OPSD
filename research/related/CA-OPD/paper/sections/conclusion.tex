\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state the boundaries of our claims plainly.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Final quality not improved.} On this benchmark CA-OPD does not
  beat uniform DiffusionOPD on final per-axis quality (within single-seed noise on
  every axis, higher on none). Its demonstrated benefit is convergence efficiency
  on the lagging axis and $\tau$-controllability, not final quality.
  \item \textbf{Near-saturated, low-conflict benchmark.} The three teachers
  (GenEval, OCR, Aesthetics) are already near their individual ceilings and only
  mildly conflict, leaving little frontier for min-max to relocate along. The
  theory predicts larger gains for \emph{higher-conflict} teacher sets, which we
  did not have.
  \item \textbf{Statistical strength.} Results are single-seed at $n{=}256$
  prompts; the OCR trade ($-0.012$) is within eval noise, and a confounding GenEval
  rise muddies the clean ``gain-aesthetics/pay-OCR'' story. We deliberately report
  \emph{per-axis} metrics only: a single min-max--normalized average is
  normalization-dependent (not comparable across papers) and partially circular
  with a min-max method (\S\ref{sec:th-conv}). Multi-seed runs with per-axis error
  bars are needed for significance.
  \item \textbf{No convergence rate.} CA-OPD's EMA-softmax weights are
  nonstationary; we prove only a conditional Pareto-stationarity guarantee (if the
  weights converge), not a rate (\S\ref{sec:th-conv}).
  \item \textbf{Absolute-loss weighting risk.} Weighting by absolute EMA-KL can
  chase an irreducible or reward-hacked teacher. Here the up-weighted axis
  (aesthetics) was reducible, so the pathology did not bite, but it is a real
  failure mode.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We revisited multi-teacher on-policy distillation through multi-objective
optimization. Our main contribution is theoretical: multi-teacher OPD losses are
one commensurable functional in a shared isotropic output metric; the uniform
(teacher-mean) student is Pareto-optimal \emph{in loss}, so the real difficulty
is the nonlinear loss$\to$reward map under which the loss-sum minimizer can be
reward-dominated; and an adaptive Tchebycheff min-max reweighting whose detached
weights give the \emph{exact} surrogate gradient (envelope theorem) is a
principled response, with mopd as its $\tau\to\infty$ limit. Empirically, on a
faithful, triple-validated reproduction of DiffusionOPD, our CA-OPD improves
convergence efficiency on the lagging reward axis and exposes $\tau$ as a
speed-vs-ceiling knob, while an honest four-idea ablation maps the OPD-improvement
design space (teacher reweighting is safe; per-step and sampler manipulation
hurt). We do not claim a new state of the art: on this near-saturated benchmark
CA-OPD does not raise final per-axis quality.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The theory points to two concrete next steps: (i) \textbf{higher-conflict teacher
sets}, where min-max frontier selection should yield genuine final-quality gains,
not just speed; and (ii) \textbf{regret-based weighting}
$r_m=\mathcal L_m-\mathrm{floor}_m$, weighting the \emph{reducible} gap to a
teacher's attainable floor rather than absolute loss, to avoid chasing
irreducible teachers. A two-timescale analysis of the EMA-softmax dynamics, or an
inner MGDA/CAGrad solve inheriting existing guarantees, would close the
convergence-rate gap.
